The acceptance probability for report $r$ given prior belief $b$ is
%
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{accept}) = \frac{D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\delta}}{|r-b|^{\delta}+D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\delta}},
  \tag{S1}
\end{equation}
%
where $D_{\mathrm{eff}} = D\cdot(1+0.5\,T_{\mathrm{source}})$ for social
contacts and $D_{\mathrm{eff}}=D$ for the AI.
This generalises the classical bounded-confidence model \citep{deffuant2000mixing} by
replacing the binary threshold with a smooth curve parameterised by $(D,\delta)$.

\subsection*{S3\quad AI Alignment Mechanism}

The AI response formula is
%
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{AI}} = (1-\alpha)\,t + \alpha\,b,
  \tag{S2}
\end{equation}
%
where $t$ is the ground-truth cell severity, $b$ is the querying agent's current
belief, and $\alpha\in[0,1]$ is the alignment parameter.
At $\alpha=0$: $r_{\mathrm{AI}}=t$ (fully truthful).
At $\alpha=1$: $r_{\mathrm{AI}}=b$ (perfect confirmation).
Intermediate values produce a weighted average --- the AI is partially informative
and partially sycophantic.

This linear interpolation is the simplest formulation spanning the
truth--confirmation spectrum without additional free parameters.
Non-linear or stochastic variants (e.g.\
$r\sim\mathcal{N}(\alpha b+(1-\alpha)t,\,\sigma^2)$) were not pursued in the
main sweep to preserve interpretability; adding symmetric noise shifts all
SECI/AECI curves uniformly without displacing $\alpha^*$.

\subsection*{S4\quad Cognitive Gap Scalar}

The gap scalar $g$ parameterises cognitive divergence between agent types around
a shared midpoint:
%
\begin{align}
  D_{\mathrm{exploit}}(g) &= \max(3.0 - 1.0\,g,\;0.1),
  &\delta_{\mathrm{exploit}}(g) &= 2.35 + 1.15\,g, \tag{S3}\\
  D_{\mathrm{explor}}(g)  &= 3.0 + 1.0\,g,
  &\delta_{\mathrm{explor}}(g)  &= \max(2.35 - 1.15\,g,\;0.1). \tag{S4}
\end{align}

\centering
\caption{Cognitive parameters at each gap-scalar value.}
\label{tab:S4}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
$g$ & $D_{\mathrm{exploit}}$ & $\delta_{\mathrm{exploit}}$ & $D_{\mathrm{explor}}$ & $\delta_{\mathrm{explor}}$ \\
\midrule
0.0 & 3.00 & 2.35 & 3.00 & 2.35 \\
0.5 & 2.50 & 2.93 & 3.50 & 1.78 \\
1.0 & 2.00 & 3.50 & 4.00 & 1.20 \\
1.5 & 1.50 & 4.08 & 4.50 & 0.63 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The shared midpoint ($D_{\mathrm{mid}}=3.0$, $\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}=2.35$) is
the arithmetic mean of the baseline exploit/explor values.
At $g=0$ both types are cognitively identical, providing a null condition for
heterogeneity effects.
The invariant $D_{\mathrm{exploit}}<D_{\mathrm{explor}}$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm{exploit}}>\delta_{\mathrm{explor}}$ holds for all $g>0$,
ensuring the exploitative type always has a narrower and steeper acceptance curve.

The gap sweep extends the design to a second axis: $d_{\mathrm{mid}}\in\{2.0,3.0,4.0\}$
independently varies the absolute level of cognitive openness (closed, baseline, open)
orthogonally to the inter-type gap $g$.
The full 2D sweep uses $N=20$ independently seeded replications per $(g,d_{\mathrm{mid}},\alpha)$
cell and $T=200$ ticks, matching the primary alignment sweep.

\subsection*{S5\quad Metric Definitions}

\subsubsection*{S5.1\enspace Social Echo Chamber Index (SECI)}

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SECI}_c =
  \begin{cases}
    \max\!\left(-1,\;\dfrac{V_c - V_g}{V_g}\right)
      & V_c < V_g, \\[8pt]
    \min\!\left(+1,\;\dfrac{V_c - V_g}{5 - V_g}\right)
      & V_c \geq V_g,
  \tag{S5}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
%
where $V_c$ is the belief variance within connected component $c$ (member beliefs
with severity level $\geq1$) and $V_g$ the corresponding global variance across
all agents.
Component values are averaged within each agent type every five ticks
(the network is type-homogeneous).
Negative values indicate an echo chamber; this sign convention is shared by all
echo-chamber indices.
A \emph{formation} event is registered when $\mathrm{SECI}<-0.30$ sustained for
$\geq3$ consecutive samples; a \emph{recovery} event when
$\mathrm{SECI}\geq0.0$ sustained for $\geq5$ consecutive samples after formation.
If formation never occurs the lifecycle bar is left blank ($\circ$); if formation
occurs but recovery does not before the run ends the bar reaches maximum height
with annotation $\times$.

\subsubsection*{S5.2\enspace AI Echo Chamber Indices (AECI-Var, AECI-Err, AECI-Acc)}

\paragraph{AECI-Var.} Applies the SECI formula (S5) with grouping by AI reliance:
within each agent type, agents are median-split by cumulative accepted AI belief
updates, the belief variance of each type's AI-reliant half replaces $V_c$, and
the two type values are averaged (the within-type split avoids conflating AI's
effect on beliefs with type self-selection into AI use).
Negative = AI-induced bubble; this construct enters the Goldilocks composite.

\paragraph{AECI-Err.} Compares confidence-weighted belief error between the
AI-heavy and AI-light halves (same median split):
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AECI\text{-}Err}
    = -\,\frac{\bar e_{\mathrm{heavy}} - \bar e_{\mathrm{light}}}
              {\max(\bar e_{\mathrm{heavy}}, \bar e_{\mathrm{light}})},
  \qquad
  \bar e = \overline{\text{conf}\times|b-t|}\ \text{over cells believed at } L\geq1.
  \tag{S6a}
\end{equation}
%
Negative values mean AI-heavy agents are more confidently wrong (algorithmic echo
chamber).
\emph{Sign history:} before 2026-07 this index used the opposite sign; result
files written before the change are automatically converted on load via the
\texttt{conventions.aeci\_err\_sign} marker.

\paragraph{AECI-Acc.}
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AECI\text{-}Acc} = \frac{\Delta q_{\mathrm{AI}}}
                       {\Delta q_{\mathrm{AI}} + \Delta q_{\mathrm{human}}},
  \tag{S6b}
\end{equation}
%
where $\Delta q$ denotes the increment in \emph{accepted} information-update
calls over a five-tick window --- an unsigned $[0,1]$ reliance share, not an
echo-chamber index.

\subsubsection*{S5.3\enspace Belief Accuracy (MAE)}

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{C}}|b_{i,k}-t_k|,
  \tag{S7}
\end{equation}
%
averaged over agents $i$, where $\mathcal{C}=\{k:t_k\geq1\}$ is the set of
cells with non-zero true severity.

\subsubsection*{S5.4\enspace Composite Goldilocks Scores and $\alpha^*$ Sensitivity}

Let $\bar{m}(\alpha)$ denote the steady-state mean (last 75 ticks) of metric $m$
at alignment level $\alpha$.
Range normalisation across the sweep:
%
\begin{equation}
  m_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha) =
    \frac{\bar{m}(\alpha)-\min_{\alpha}\bar{m}}
         {\max_{\alpha}\bar{m}-\min_{\alpha}\bar{m}}.
  \tag{S8}
\end{equation}
%
Composite scores:
%
\begin{align}
  \text{total\_bubble}_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha)
    &= |\mathrm{SECI}|_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha)
       +|\mathrm{AECI\text{-}Var}|_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha), \tag{S9}\\
  \text{total\_score}_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha)
    &= |\mathrm{SECI}|_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha)
       +|\mathrm{AECI\text{-}Var}|_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha)
       +\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha). \tag{S10}
\end{align}
%
Optima:
%
\begin{align}
  \alpha^* &= \arg\min_{\alpha}\,\text{total\_bubble}_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha), \tag{S11}\\
  \alpha^*(+\mathrm{MAE}) &= \arg\min_{\alpha}\,\text{total\_score}_{\mathrm{norm}}(\alpha). \tag{S12}
\end{align}
%
Range normalisation ensures each component contributes on the same $[0,1]$ scale
regardless of the absolute magnitude of SECI, AECI-Var, or MAE.
The two optima are reported separately so that the trade-off between
echo-chamber suppression and accuracy improvement is explicit.
Because the raw AECI-Var signal is small relative to SECI, normalisation can
amplify its influence on the location of the minimum; $\alpha^*$ is therefore
also reported under six composite variants ---
$\{$SECI+AECI-Var, SECI only, SECI+AECI-Err$\}\times\{$without MAE, with MAE$\}$
--- and treated as robust only if the variants agree.

\subsubsection*{S5.5\enspace Spatial and Network Periphery Metrics}

\paragraph{Spatial periphery.}
For each simulation run, cells are ranked by Euclidean distance to that run's
epicentre.
\emph{Near} cells (Q1, closest quartile) and \emph{far} cells (Q4, furthest
quartile) are identified per run; coverage deficit (actual severity minus mean
aid tokens per tick) and aid density are averaged within each set and then
averaged across replications.
This per-run computation avoids the blurring artefact that would arise from
projecting all replications onto a single grid when epicentre locations vary.

\paragraph{Network periphery.}
Agents are ranked by betweenness centrality on the type-homogeneous community
graph (degree-quartile scalars are also recorded).
Low-betweenness agents (Q1) and high-betweenness ``brokers'' (Q4) are compared
on belief MAE and aid tokens sent.
Both agent-level comparisons are reported as steady-state means (last 75 ticks)
of five-tick-cadence series: belief MAE over disaster cells (L1+, the same
construct as the headline MAE) and aid tokens sent per agent per five-tick
window, counted at placement time.
Figures show 95\% confidence intervals over the $N=20$ replications, and the
full time evolution of the within-run paired gaps (periphery $-$ core) is
reported separately.

\paragraph{Interpretation caveat.}
The periphery gaps are structurally bounded in this model: agents are immobile
and sense only their own cell, while query targeting, AI sensing (uniform
random 15\% of the grid per tick), and relief targeting all operate on the full
grid independently of agent position; social communities are small
($\sim$25 agents) and densely wired ($p=0.7$, diameter $\approx 2$), and humans
may query any other human (non-friends at a small baseline weight), so neither
spawn location nor network position gates information access strongly.
The gap metrics therefore quantify residual inequity from local sensing and
initial-belief differences, not from differential access to communication
channels.

Three regularities emerge (main-text Fig.~7, Table~\ref{tab:S7b}). First,
cognitive heterogeneity -- or uniform closed-mindedness -- is a precondition
for the interior optimum: in cognitively homogeneous populations ($g = 0$)
with baseline or open acceptance ($d_{\mathrm{mid}} \geq 3.0$), the optimal
alignment is $\alpha^{*} = 0$, i.e., a fully truthful AI; the interior optimum
emerges once confirmation-seeking agents are present ($g \geq 0.5$), or when
the entire population is closed ($d_{\mathrm{mid}} = 2.0$, where
$\alpha^{*} = 0.7$ even at $g = 0$). Second, $\alpha^{*}$(bubble) increases
with both the inter-type gap and closedness, saturating at 0.8 for closed or
strongly polarised populations, while open populations
($d_{\mathrm{mid}} = 4.0$) keep $\alpha^{*}$ at 0.3--0.4 at every gap level;
operational optima lie at or below the bubble optima in every cell, consistent
with the structural account in the main text. Third, the cost paid \emph{at
the optimum} rises along both axes: belief error at $\alpha^{*}$ grows from
1.12 (open, homogeneous) to 1.63 (closed, polarised) and the social echo
chamber deepens from $-0.14$ to $-0.24$ -- populations that require more
confirmation to accept information are worse off even under their own optimal
policy. The baseline cell reproduces the primary sweep's bubble optimum
($\alpha^{*} = 0.8$) and places the operational optimum at 0.4, inside the
primary analysis's 0.3--0.5 range -- an independent consistency check from a
separate 2{,}640-run batch.

\subsection*{S8\quad Factor Sensitivity Sweeps}

Three one-at-a-time factor sweeps test the robustness of the findings to the
information environment and population composition. All use $\alpha = 0.5$ and
$N = 20$ replications with run length $T/2 = 100$ ticks, under the main-model
configuration (workflow run 32113892791). Because the factor runs cover 100
ticks, they characterise the \emph{formation phase} of the response rather
than the steady state of the primary sweep. Note that the base parameterisation
holds rumour probability at 1.0, so the disaster-dynamics and
exploitative-share conditions are evaluated in the harshest rumour
environment.

Disaster dynamics mode controls the rate at which cell severities drift and
receive shocks: mode~0 fixes the initial map; mode~2 applies the baseline shock

The formation phase is dominated by the rumour environment
(Fig.~\ref{fig:S7fig}). Removing rumours entirely (probability 0: no community
starts from a shared false narrative) reduces cumulative unmet high-need
events over the first 100 ticks from 888 to 265 and lifts early targeting
precision from 0.23 to 0.79, while tick-averaged belief error falls from 1.77
to 1.66. The social-chamber response differs by agent type: under universal
rumours, exploitative communities' insularity nearly vanishes ($-0.25$ to
$-0.05$, as the conflict between rumour and observation raises
within-community belief variance) while exploratory communities deepen
($-0.15$ to $-0.28$). Disaster tempo leaves all outcomes essentially unchanged
(cumulative unmet events 829--882 from a static to a rapidly changing hazard),
indicating that the mid-range findings are not an artefact of hazard dynamics.
Raising the exploitative share from 0.2 to 0.8 deepens the population-average
social chamber ($-0.08$ to $-0.24$) and increases cumulative unmet events by
roughly 30\% (815 to 1050). Together with S7, this locates the population-side
driver of harm in confirmation-seeking composition and shared false
narratives, not in the hazard itself.

\subsection*{S9\quad Software and Reproducibility}

The model is implemented in Python~3.11 using Mesa~3.x for agent scheduling,
NetworkX for social-network construction, and NumPy / Matplotlib for numerical
computation and visualisation.
All simulation runs are seeded via \texttt{numpy.random.seed(run\_index)},
ensuring bit-for-bit reproducibility given the same Python and library versions.
Results are serialised to JSON after each $(\alpha,\mathrm{run})$ cell; the
plotting pipeline reads these files without re-running the simulation, so figures
can be regenerated without recomputation.
